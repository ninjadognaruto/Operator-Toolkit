TurboTax Expert Assist — Home Page Optimization

March 21, 2026  ·  Product: Do Your Taxes With Expert Help  ·  Analysis: Copy, UX, Qual Research, Competitive

The Expert Assist home page has a pricing transparency and trust gap that competitors are actively exploiting. Users arrive with tax-filing anxiety — they want confidence, not ambiguity. The page fails to answer the two questions every visitor has before they convert: What will this cost me? and What happens when I click "Start for free"? H&R Block has made "No Surprise Guarantee" a brand pillar. FreeTaxUSA and TaxAct display explicit pricing tiers and credential signals at a fraction of TurboTax's price point. Fixing the hero, the pricing signal, and the post-CTA continuity are low-effort, high-impact changes that require no engineering work — just copy and design.

Context

This analysis covers the Expert Assist product landing page — TurboTax's hybrid "do-it-yourself with expert guidance" offering, positioned between DIY and Expert Full Service. The page serves as the primary top-of-funnel conversion surface for users considering the product. The recording captures the full scrollable page including hero, feature sections, trust signals, testimonials, FAQs, and the post-CTA sign-up flow.

The goal is to identify opportunities that reduce bounce and improve conversion to "Start for free," with a secondary focus on reducing post-CTA drop-off. Quantitative data was not available for this round. Findings are grounded in copy/UX analysis (see deep dive), Reddit/community forum qualitative research, and competitive analysis across H&R Block, FreeTaxUSA, and TaxAct (see deep dive).

Customer Problems

1. Pricing opacity before commitment
I am a price-conscious filer evaluating TurboTax Expert Assist and I'm trying to understand what I'll pay before I commit
But I can't find a clear price on the page or understand when "free" ends
Because
  • The hero CTA says "Start for free" with no pricing context below it
  • Pricing only becomes visible at the end of the filing process
  • FAQ questions about fees and pricing are buried seven sections below the fold
Which makes me feel anxious and distrustful — I suspect there's a catch
Qual evidence:
"A trap all the way until filing — discovering how much it costs right at the end." — TurboTax community forum
"I was charged for TurboTax Online Expert Assist Premium 2025. I didn't request these services, nor was I provided them." — TurboTax community forum
Competitive context: H&R Block runs attack ads on this exact gap ("Pay less than TurboTax when you need live expert help"). TaxAct shows a price ($45) on the Xpert Assist landing page. FreeTaxUSA shows a 3-tier pricing table ($0 / $7.99 / $44.99). TurboTax is the outlier in opacity.
2. Product confusion between Expert Assist and Expert Full Service
I am a first-time Expert Assist visitor and I'm trying to decide whether this is the right product for my tax situation
But I can't distinguish Expert Assist from Expert Full Service without scrolling to the FAQ
Because
  • Both products appear on the same page without a comparison table or clear decision aid
  • The Expert Full Service cross-sell appears mid-page without context about who it's for
  • The only clarification is FAQ #1, which requires scrolling six sections to find
Which makes me feel confused and uncertain — I'm not sure I'm on the right product
Page evidence: The first FAQ on the page is "What are the differences between Expert Assist and Expert Full Service?" — this question existing at all indicates the page creates the confusion it then tries to resolve.
3. Hero doesn't speak to my actual fear state
I am a filer with a complicated tax year (freelance income, investments, a major life event) and I'm trying to determine if Expert Assist can handle my specific situation
But the page leads with aspiration ("file confidently") rather than addressing the fears that brought me here
Because
  • The headline names an outcome (confidence) instead of mirroring the user's starting anxiety
  • No mention of the specific situations — freelance, investments, life events — that make Expert Assist the right choice
  • "Experts by your side" doesn't specify what the expert can actually help with
Which makes me feel unseen — this copy could be for anyone, so I'm not sure it's for me
Qual evidence:
"I was very concerned about all the new things I needed to do this year. Opening my own business, investing in stocks... the list was huge. TurboTax helped walk me through filing." — TurboTax Expert Assist verified reviewer
This reviewer converted — but the page never spoke to their fear. The opportunity is to mirror it proactively.
4. Conversion momentum breaks the moment I click "Start for free"
I am a new user who just clicked "Start for free" and I'm trying to set up my Expert Assist account
But I land on a page that says "Create an Intuit Account" with no mention of Expert Assist
Because
  • The post-CTA screen uses Intuit branding, not TurboTax or Expert Assist branding
  • There is no acknowledgment of what I just signed up for or what comes next
  • The headline "Let's get you in" is generic and disconnected from the Expert Assist product promise
Which makes me feel uncertain and second-guessing — I wonder if I clicked the wrong button
Framework evidence: This is a Certainty (Control) gap. Per Self-Determination Theory, users need to know what just happened and what comes next. The post-CTA screen provides neither.
5. Expert credentialing is asserted but not proven
I am a filer weighing whether to pay a premium for expert help and I'm trying to verify that TurboTax's experts are qualified to handle my situation
But TurboTax makes vague claims while competitors name specific credentials
Because
  • The page only states "average of 13 years of experience" — no credential types (CPA, EA, Tax Attorney)
  • Forum users report some "experts" are product support agents, not tax professionals
  • TaxAct and FreeTaxUSA explicitly state "100% U.S.-based, credentialed" as headline claims
Which makes me feel skeptical about whether the premium price is justified
Qual evidence:
"When you connect with an expert, it is NOT a tax expert — it is a product expert. The agents are trained only on the product and cannot advise on most tax questions." — TurboTax review, SuperMoney
Competitive context: TaxAct: "100% U.S.-based, credentialed tax experts." FreeTaxUSA: lists CPAs and EAs explicitly. TurboTax's credentialing copy is the weakest of the four competitors analyzed.

Hypotheses

H1 — Pricing transparency reduces hesitation at the primary CTA
If we make pricing expectations clear before the user commits to starting
Then users will enter the funnel with their cost anxiety resolved rather than deferred
resulting in higher CTA click-through rate and lower pricing-related support ticket volume
Which we will measure by CTA click-through rate in an A/B test (4-week run), and pricing-related support ticket volume month-over-month
H2 — Addressing user anxiety in the hero improves scroll depth and CTA conversion
If we reframe the hero messaging to acknowledge the user's anxiety before resolving it
Then users with complicated tax situations will recognize Expert Assist as built for them
resulting in lower bounce rate, higher scroll depth, and higher CTA click-through
Which we will measure by bounce rate, scroll depth past the hero section, and CTA CTR in an A/B test
H3 — Helping users self-select the right product removes the top source of page confusion
If we make the distinction between Expert Assist and Expert Full Service clear without requiring users to seek it out
Then users will arrive at the CTA confident they are on the right product
resulting in lower FAQ click-through rate and higher Expert Assist conversion
Which we will measure by FAQ #1 click rate, CTA CTR, and scroll-to-CTA completion rate
H4 — Maintaining brand continuity through sign-up reduces post-click drop-off
If we reinforce what the user signed up for immediately after they click the primary CTA
Then users will feel confirmed rather than redirected, and complete account creation at a higher rate
resulting in higher sign-up funnel completion rate
Which we will measure by sign-up funnel completion rate from CTA click to account created (pre vs. post change)
H5 — Surfacing social validation at the conversion moment increases CTA click-through
If we improve social validation from other customers at the point of highest decision anxiety
Then users will feel less alone in their choice and more willing to commit
resulting in improved CTA click-through rate
Which we will measure by CTA CTR in an A/B test (4-week run), measured against current placement

A. Overall UX Health

Full copy and UX analysis from Steps 2a and 2b. See also: Step 2a Copy Analysis Report · Step 2b UX Analysis Report

Copy 🔴
  • Hero headline fails Copy Swap Test — any competitor could use it
  • Value prop ("100% accuracy—guaranteed") repeats verbatim three times
  • Pricing anxiety never addressed proactively anywhere on the page
  • "You can trust our experts" asserts trust without providing evidence
  • Post-CTA screen uses Intuit branding — full brand disconnect
  • "Unlimited personalized help" uses two generic, untestable modifiers
Layout 🟡
  • Content alternates sides across sections, disrupting the F-pattern scan path
  • "Start for free" CTA appears 6+ times with zero contextual variation
  • FAQ groups three distinct categories (product, process, pricing) in a flat undifferentiated list
  • 3-step bullets and phone mockup animation tell different stories simultaneously
Emphasis 🟡
  • Primary and secondary hero CTAs have near-equal visual weight — neither wins the Foggy Glasses Test
  • 4.3/5 stars and 24,300 reviews are buried mid-page, far from the conversion moment
  • Three guarantee badges are identical in size and weight — no hierarchy despite different value claims
  • Phone mockup animations are too small for in-mockup text to be readable
Accessibility 🔴
  • "Start for free" doesn't communicate what clicking actually does — creates hesitation at the conversion moment
  • Expert Assist vs. Expert Full Service appear on the same page with no decision aid
  • "Guaranteed" hyperlink is inline and small — high-anxiety users who most need it are least likely to find it
  • Sign-up screen requests both email and phone before delivering any product value
Reward 🔴
  • Pricing anxiety (dominant user emotion) is unaddressed throughout the entire page
  • Post-CTA drop to Intuit sign-up screen removes all reward for committing — the certainty signal vanishes
  • No completion state or success visualization anywhere — users can't picture what "done" looks like

  • "Tax Return Lifetime Guarantee" badge is opaque — high-anxiety users will leave to search for what it means

B. Qualitative Research — Reddit & Forums

Research sourced from TurboTax community forums (ttlc.intuit.com), Reddit, and third-party review platforms (SuperMoney, Claimyr, JustAnswer). All quotes are verbatim from public forum posts.

Theme 1: Pricing shock and unexpected charges

"I was charged for TurboTax Online Expert Assist Premium 2025. I didn't request these services, nor was I provided them. Can you remove the $159 charge?"
"A trap all the way until filing — discovering how much it costs right at the end."
"I paid $119 in 2023 and $129 in 2024. Now I'm being charged $209 for Live Premium I don't remember agreeing to."

Multiple users report charges of $159–$209 they did not knowingly choose. The gap between "Start for free" and the final bill is the single most common complaint across all sources.

Theme 2: Forced enrollment and difficult downgrade

"How do I switch from Expert Assist to Do it yourself? I didn't want or choose this upcharge."
"TurboTax forced me to accept Expert Services because I checked the box associated with tuition payments."

Users report being defaulted into Expert Assist when they check certain tax situation boxes (tuition, self-employment, etc.). Once enrolled, the DIY options appear grayed out — downgrading requires knowing exactly where to click.

Theme 3: Expert quality inconsistency

"When you connect with an expert, it is NOT a tax expert — it is a product expert. The agents are trained only on the product and cannot advise on most tax questions." — SuperMoney review

A meaningful minority of users encountered product support agents rather than credentialed tax professionals. This is both a product trust issue and a credentialing transparency gap the page copy does not address.

Theme 4: Positive experiences focus on the hand-holding value

"TurboTax Live Expert Carol was very helpful and effective. She helped me ensure with confidence that everything was filed correctly." — TurboTax Expert Assist verified review
"Friends had recommended TurboTax to me and I'm so glad I took their advice... I love that you can ask questions throughout and get your answers as if a tax expert was right there beside you." — TurboTax verified review

When the experience works as intended, users articulate the core value proposition clearly: having an expert present throughout the filing process, not just at the end. The page currently underplays this "always there" quality.

C. Competitive Analysis

Analysis covers expert-assisted offerings only across three direct competitors. Pricing and feature claims sourced from competitor websites and third-party reviews (NerdWallet, Kiplinger, The College Investor).

H&R Block — Tax Pro Review
  • Expert help bundled into all paid tiers — no upgrade required
  • Tax Pro formal sign-off available as add-on at ~$55
  • "No Surprise Guarantee": upfront pricing, transparent process, free audit assistance
  • Review turnaround: within 3 business days
  • Active attack ads targeting TurboTax: "Pay less than TurboTax when you need live expert help"
So what: H&R Block has made TurboTax's pricing opacity a competitive weapon. Every day the Expert Assist page lacks pricing transparency is another day H&R Block's ad spend is more effective.
FreeTaxUSA — Pro Support
  • 3-tier pricing displayed transparently: $0 / $7.99 / $44.99
  • Pro Support ($44.99): phone + live screen share with a tax professional
  • Available from the free tier — no base product upgrade required
  • Screen sharing prominently featured on product page
  • 4.8/5 stars — outscoring TurboTax's 4.3/5
So what: At $44.99 for phone + screen share vs. TurboTax's $159–$209, the value gap is hard to defend unless brand justifies the premium. For price-sensitive filers, this comparison is the clearest reason to switch.
TaxAct — Xpert Assist
  • Starting price of ~$45 displayed on the landing page
  • "100% U.S.-based, credentialed tax experts" as headline trust signal
  • $100,000 accuracy guarantee — more concrete than TurboTax's badge seals
  • Xpress Connect: 20-minute focused session option for quick questions
  • 32,930+ reviews displayed on page
  • Flexible access: chat or schedule a call, with screen sharing
So what: TaxAct wins on credentialing specificity and guarantee concreteness. "100% U.S.-based, credentialed" directly addresses the quality anxiety TurboTax's copy leaves unresolved. The $100K number is a more specific commitment than any of TurboTax's three badge seals.

D. Top 10 Opportunities

Combined and prioritized across copy analysis (Step 2a), UX analysis (Step 2b), qualitative research (Step 3), and competitive research (Step 5).

1Rewrite the hero headline to a specific, fear-resolving outcome
HighCopy / WIIFM
Current: "File confidently with experts by your side." Fails the Copy Swap Test — any competitor could use it. Direction: mirror the fear state, then resolve it. E.g., "Don't guess. An expert checks your return before you file." Validated by qual research: users describe fear-driven entry points that the page never acknowledges.
2Add pricing transparency directly beneath the primary CTA
HighCopy + UX / Safety
E.g., "Start free — see your price before you file. Pay only when you're done." H&R Block is running attack campaigns on this exact gap. Multiple forum users report $159–$209 pricing shock. Copy-only fix, zero engineering cost.
3Move the 4.3/5 star rating to the hero, adjacent to the primary CTA
HighUX / Placement
24,300 reviews and a 4.3-star rating currently sit five scrolls below the fold. Social proof reduces hesitation most at the moment of commitment — not in a dedicated reviews section. Layout change only.
4Replace the Expert Full Service cross-sell with a product comparison table
HighUX / Clarity
FAQ #1 is "What are the differences between Expert Assist and Expert Full Service?" — the page creates the confusion it then tries to resolve. A simple two-column table eliminates the need for that FAQ entry entirely.
5Bridge the post-CTA sign-up screen back to Expert Assist
HighCopy + UX / Certainty
Replace "Let's get you in" / "Create an Intuit Account" with "Set up your Expert Assist account — your expert is ready when you are." The Intuit-branded screen reads as a redirect to a different product. This is the Certainty (Control) gap: users don't know what just happened or what comes next.
6Rewrite "Unlimited personalized help" to lead with nights/weekends availability
HighCopy / WIIFM
E.g., "A tax expert on call — nights, weekends, all season." "Unlimited personalized help" fails both the Copy Swap Test and the Falsifiability Test. The nights-and-weekends detail is the genuine differentiator and it's currently buried in the subhead.
7Lead the expert credentialing section with the 13-year stat, not a self-assertion
MediumCopy / Trust
Replace "You can trust our experts" with "Average 13 years of tax experience. Trained on every law change." TaxAct leads with "100% U.S.-based, credentialed." The proof is already in the subhead — it just needs to move up.
8Consolidate the repeated value prop — eliminate the three-peat of "100% accuracy—guaranteed"
MediumCopy / Brevity
Map a clear message architecture: hero = outcome, expert review section = the mechanism, guarantee section = the proof. Each section should add new information. Repetition without escalation trains users to skim.
9Add one fear-resonant testimonial that mirrors the target user's anxiety state
MediumUX / Recognition
All three current testimonials are 5-star generic praise. Fear-resolution stories convert skeptical buyers. E.g., a first-time filer who had an LLC, a filer post-divorce, someone with 1099 + W-2 income — these mirror the actual audience for Expert Assist.
10Explain the guarantee badges in plain language on the page
MediumCopy + UX / Safety
"Tax Return Lifetime Guarantee" is opaque as a badge label. High-anxiety users — exactly those who scroll to the guarantee section — will leave to search for what it means rather than click "Start for free." TaxAct's $100K accuracy number is a more concrete commitment than any of TurboTax's three seals.
