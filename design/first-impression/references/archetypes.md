# Brand Archetypes — Mark & Pearson's 12 Jung-derived archetypes

From Margaret Mark and Carol Pearson's *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2002). Every brand archetype carries a recognizable visual / tonal signature that the visceral read picks up before conscious analysis. Use this reference to force a concrete archetype pick during the First Impression skill.

## How to apply

1. From the 12 archetypes below, **pick 1 primary archetype** that the design viscerally reads as.
2. If the design has mixed signals, **pick a secondary** (1-2 max).
3. For each pick, **cite the specific visual signal** that creates the read — not vague vibes.
4. Compare to the brand's **stated positioning**. Misalignment is the diagnostic.

## The two axes

Mark & Pearson organized the 12 archetypes on two axes:

- **Stability ↔ Mastery / Risk** — does the brand promise belonging and predictability, or change and challenge?
- **Belonging ↔ Independence** — is the brand about the group, or about the individual self?

Quadrants:
- **Stability + Belonging**: Caregiver, Innocent, Everyman
- **Stability + Independence**: Sage, Creator, Ruler
- **Mastery + Belonging**: Lover, Jester
- **Mastery + Independence**: Hero, Outlaw, Magician, Explorer

(There's some overlap; the quadrants aren't rigid.)

## The 12 archetypes

### 1. Sage

**Core promise**: truth, knowledge, mastery through understanding.
**Personality**: thoughtful, expert, unflashy, relies on credibility.
**Visual signature**:
- Restrained, often muted color palette (navy, deep grey, parchment)
- Serif typography common (NYT, Wikipedia)
- Dense type, heavy text-to-whitespace ratio
- Footnotes, citations, "library" aesthetic
- Photography is documentary or absent; illustration is editorial

**Digital examples**: Bloomberg, NYT, Wikipedia, Wolfram, Britannica, Stratechery

**Misalignment risk**: brands that *want* to feel scrappy or innovative end up reading as Sage when they over-rely on dense type and restrained color.

---

### 2. Hero

**Core promise**: courage, achievement, mastery through effort.
**Personality**: bold, action-oriented, confident, slightly intimidating.
**Visual signature**:
- High-contrast (often black + bold accent)
- Large, bold sans-serif typography (Inter, Helvetica Bold, custom display)
- Action photography (athletes, builders, doers)
- Imperative copy ("Just do it" / "Build" / "Launch")
- Bold CTAs, no apologetic language

**Digital examples**: Nike, Stripe, Tesla, Linear, Vercel

**Misalignment risk**: friendly consumer products styled as Hero come across as aggressive. Hero archetype demands the brand actually delivers performance.

---

### 3. Outlaw

**Core promise**: revolution, breaking the rules, shaking the establishment.
**Personality**: rebellious, irreverent, edgy.
**Visual signature**:
- Off-color palettes (acid green + black, hot pink + grey)
- Punk / brutalist typography (custom, irregular, distorted)
- Breaking conventional grids deliberately
- Copy that mocks competitors or category norms
- Often monochrome with one shocking accent

**Digital examples**: Liquid Death, Cash App, Vice, Doc Martens, Ramp (early)

**Misalignment risk**: enterprise B2B products can't sustain Outlaw — the visceral signal undermines trust. Reserve for challenger brands willing to alienate the conservative half of their market.

---

### 4. Magician

**Core promise**: vision, transformation, making the impossible possible.
**Personality**: visionary, premium, slightly mysterious.
**Visual signature**:
- Dark mode by default, often with dramatic gradients
- Glow effects, light leaks, atmospheric photography
- Premium minimalism — lots of negative space
- Future-tense copy ("Imagine...", "What if...")
- Custom geometric or futurist typography

**Digital examples**: Apple (post-2010), Tesla, Linear, Arc Browser, Things 3

**Misalignment risk**: Magician requires craft to support the promise. Half-executed Magician (gradient + minimalism without polish) reads as pretentious or empty.

---

### 5. Creator

**Core promise**: self-expression, imagination, building something new.
**Personality**: playful, expressive, generative, slightly maximalist.
**Visual signature**:
- Bright accent colors, often used liberally
- Mixed type weights and styles (display + body together)
- Asymmetric or expressive layouts
- User-generated content showcased prominently
- Tools/canvas/palette imagery

**Digital examples**: Adobe, Figma, Notion, Canva, Procreate, Are.na

**Misalignment risk**: Creator works only when the product genuinely enables creation. Marketing-tool brands styling as Creator without supporting the promise read as inauthentic.

---

### 6. Ruler

**Core promise**: control, prestige, leadership of the category.
**Personality**: authoritative, premium, established.
**Visual signature**:
- Heavy weight typography (often serif — Bodoni, Didot, custom)
- Gold / dark / oxblood palette
- Symmetric, structured layouts with classical proportions
- "Trusted by [enterprise logos]" frames
- Architectural / institutional photography

**Digital examples**: Mercedes, Microsoft 365, BlackRock, JPMorgan, Salesforce (enterprise tier)

**Misalignment risk**: scrappy startups attempting Ruler aesthetic look like they're cosplaying enterprise. Ruler requires actual market position.

---

### 7. Caregiver

**Core promise**: care, protection, generosity.
**Personality**: warm, supportive, parental, nurturing.
**Visual signature**:
- Warm pastels, especially soft pinks, yellows, greens
- Rounded sans-serif typography (Circular, Avenir Next, Recoleta)
- Rounded UI corners, soft shadows
- Photography of people being cared for (not aspirational)
- Reassuring, plain-language copy

**Digital examples**: Headspace, Calm, Volvo, Johnson & Johnson, Charity:Water, Babylist

**Misalignment risk**: Caregiver styling on a fast-paced productivity tool feels patronizing. Caregiver works when the user is genuinely vulnerable (health, finance, parenting, grief).

---

### 8. Lover

**Core promise**: intimacy, sensuality, indulgence, beauty.
**Personality**: sensual, romantic, indulgent.
**Visual signature**:
- Sensual photography (skin, fabric, food, light)
- Romantic palettes (cream + terracotta, soft black + rose)
- Italics, scripts, or refined serifs
- Lots of negative space + slow visual pacing
- Tactile / textural / haptic visual cues

**Digital examples**: Glossier, Aesop, Net-a-Porter, Le Labo, Goop

**Misalignment risk**: Lover aesthetic on tools or utility products reads as superficial. Reserve for genuinely indulgent or sensory-product categories.

---

### 9. Jester

**Core promise**: fun, joy, lightness.
**Personality**: playful, irreverent, witty.
**Visual signature**:
- Bright, primary-color palettes (often with off-rule combinations)
- Playful illustration (characters, mascots, anthropomorphism)
- Humorous copy, puns, unexpected interactions
- Wonky typography, mixed scripts
- Easter eggs and micro-delights

**Digital examples**: Mailchimp, Old Spice, Slack, Duolingo, Ramp Mascot animations

**Misalignment risk**: Jester on serious-stakes products (legal, medical, financial) reads as flippant. Jester should never apply when the user is anxious about the outcome.

---

### 10. Innocent

**Core promise**: goodness, simplicity, optimism, purity.
**Personality**: hopeful, wholesome, simple.
**Visual signature**:
- Soft palette, often pastel or earthy
- Simple, geometric shapes
- Optimistic, uncluttered copy
- Sun / sky / nature imagery
- Sans-serif with generous letter-spacing

**Digital examples**: Coca-Cola, Dove, Airbnb (early), Calendly, Kiva

**Misalignment risk**: Innocent on premium or luxury products reads as childish. Innocent on technically sophisticated products understates capability.

---

### 11. Explorer

**Core promise**: freedom, adventure, authenticity.
**Personality**: rugged, individualistic, outdoor.
**Visual signature**:
- Earthy palette (forest green, terracotta, ochre, slate)
- Photography of landscapes, expeditions, gear
- Heavy, slightly rough typography (often serif)
- Texture-rich (grain, weathered surfaces)
- Long-form storytelling about journeys

**Digital examples**: REI, Patagonia, Strava, Garmin, Land Rover, Field Notes

**Misalignment risk**: Explorer styling on pure software products feels aesthetically borrowed unless the brand genuinely lives in physical-world adventure space.

---

### 12. Everyman

**Core promise**: belonging, regularness, accessibility.
**Personality**: friendly, unpretentious, approachable.
**Visual signature**:
- Mid-saturation, broadly accessible palette
- Standard sans-serif typography (no flex)
- Photography of regular people doing regular things
- Plain, conversational copy
- No premium signals; no edge

**Digital examples**: IKEA, Target, Etsy, Craigslist, Walmart, Honda

**Misalignment risk**: Everyman on premium products undermines the price point. Everyman works when the brand promise is "for everyone, not just the elite."

---

## How to use during First Impression

1. **Read the visceral signals.** Color palette, typography, imagery style, layout proportions, copy tone — what archetype's visual signature does this match?
2. **Force the pick.** Choose 1 primary. If genuinely mixed, choose a secondary (max 2).
3. **Cite the specific signals.** "Hero — black + bright orange CTA, bold sans-serif headline, action-oriented copy."
4. **Compare to stated positioning.** Ask: what does the brand *say* it is? If the visceral archetype ≠ stated positioning, that misalignment is the headline finding.

### Example output

```
Brand Archetype:
Primary:   Sage  — restrained palette, dense serif type, citation-heavy copy
Secondary: Ruler — gold accents on the CTA, "trusted by [Fortune 500]" frame

Misalignment: brand positioning says "scrappy challenger." Visceral reads
Sage + Ruler. Visual identity contradicts the positioning by ~180 degrees.
```

This is a useful diagnostic for a positioning audit. The visceral archetype carries far more weight than the stated mission text — users feel the visual identity before they read the about page.
