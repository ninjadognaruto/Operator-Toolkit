# Decision Framework

## Step 0: Route the Decision

Classify before doing anything else:

1. **What type?** Binary (yes/no) — or choosing between A and B?
2. **What's at stake?** Buying/spending, deeper yes/no, or comparative choice?

Then pick the right lenses:

| Decision type | Lenses to use |
|---|---|
| Buying or spending something | measure + reversibility |
| Yes/no with real stakes | measure + reversibility + inversion + commitment |
| Choosing between A and B | weighted approach (incorporating measure) |

---

## The Five Lenses

### 1. measure

**measure** = how long and how often a decision acts on your life. Distinct from magnitude (value at a single moment). Real value = magnitude × measure.

Tag every decision first: **high-measure** or **low-measure**.

- **High-measure:** affects you daily or weekly, for months or years — relationships, health habits, tools you use constantly, roles you'll inhabit for years.
- **Low-measure:** one-off, short-lived, easily replaced.

High-measure decisions deserve more analysis, even for small per-unit improvements — they compound. Low-measure decisions don't need much. A quick gut-check is usually enough.

---

### 2. reversibility

| Type | Definition | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Reversible, low cost | Small, easily undone, low stakes | One clean reason to do it — or don't. Max 5 minutes. |
| Reversible, high cost | Real switching cost if wrong | Run inversion. |
| Irreversible | Can't undo — major bets, legal terms, shutdowns | Run inversion + commitment test in full. Urgency is often manufactured — don't confuse it with importance. |

---

### 3. inversion

Flip the question. Forward-looking "want" thinking hides things. Inversion surfaces them.

- What will you regret not having done in 5 years?
- What's the worst that could happen — and is it recoverable?
- What are the 3 most likely ways this fails?
- What would make this a clear mistake in hindsight?
- **Obituary test:** does this path move toward or away from the life you'd want to have lived?
- **8-year-old test** *(for major life/career decisions):* what did you love before the world started grading you? Does this path move toward or away from that?

**Fear/embarrassment check:** Is the hesitation *protective* (pointing to a real downside) — or is it a signal you actually care about the outcome? Fear clustering around a decision often means it matters. The scarier a decision feels, the more it probably deserves a second look.

Inversion often resolves the decision on its own. Unrecoverable downside → usually no. Regret of not doing it outweighs the risk → usually yes.

---

### 4. commitment test

Run before recommending yes on anything high-stakes.

1. **Shit sandwich test.** Name the specific struggle — not "it'll be hard," but the actual grind, the bad days, the cost. Are you genuinely willing to endure *this*, not just attracted to the upside? Willingness to eat a shit sandwich most people won't is also a competitive advantage. Fail this → the answer is no.
2. **Absorbed values check.** Strip out peer expectations and external validation. Are you running on your own values or ones you've absorbed from others? Does the call still hold when you strip away what looks good to other people?
3. **Motivation check.** Committing because you believe in it — or because committing feels less uncomfortable than not deciding?
4. **100% check.** Willing to give this everything for the required period? Half-commitment consumes resources without generating results. Fail this → the answer is no.

Fail #1 or #4 → the answer is no.

---

### 5. weighted approach (A vs. B)

Use when choosing between two specific options.

**Step 1 — gut read first.** If you had to eliminate one option right now with no analysis — which one? Note it. It matters if scores are close.

**Step 2 — define criteria.** Start with these, adapt to the decision:
- Fit with long-term goals
- Financial outcome / cost
- Time and energy required
- Day-to-day quality of life
- Risk level
- Reversibility
- Optionality created
- Values alignment
- measure (how long / how often will this act on your life?)

For high-measure decisions: weight recurring and compounding effects more than one-time outcomes.

**Step 3 — assign weights:**
- Non-negotiable → pass/fail. Failing eliminates the option.
- High → 3×
- Medium → 2×
- Low → 1×

**Step 4 — score 1–5.** 1 = poor, 3 = neutral, 5 = strong. Calculate weighted totals.

**Step 5 — make the call.** Don't just report the math.

> "Option [X] wins. The key reason to trust this: [most decisive criterion]. Main risk: [specific concern]. If [condition] changes, revisit."

If scores are within 10%, say so — and decide based on the gut read and highest-weight criteria, not the margin.

## Output format

Present results as a Markdown table. Show only the lenses that were applied.

Traffic light key: 🟢 points toward yes / no concerns — 🟡 caution / mixed — 🔴 points toward no / serious concern

For binary yes/no decisions:

| Lens | 🚦 | Finding |
|---|:---:|---|
| measure | 🟢 | One or two sentences on what it found. |
| reversibility | 🟢 | ... |
| inversion | 🟢 | ... |
| **commitment** | 🟢 | *(overall signal — leave finding blank, subsections carry the detail)* |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;↳ shit sandwich | 🟢 | Name the specific grind. Willing to eat it? |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;↳ absorbed values | 🟢 | Stripped of peer expectations, does this still hold? |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;↳ motivation | 🟢 | Genuine belief, or just avoiding the discomfort of not deciding? |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;↳ 100% | 🟢 | Willing to go all in for the required period? |

For A vs. B decisions, use the weighted approach lens. The traffic light reflects confidence in the winner: 🟢 clear gap, 🟡 close race (within 10%) or one live concern, 🔴 both options have serious problems.

After the table:

**The call:** One clear recommendation with the key reason.

**What to watch:** The earliest signal this is wrong.
