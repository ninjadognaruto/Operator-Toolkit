# Memo Mode (Amazon)

Six-pager narrative format for product, project, and deep-meeting memos. Bezos's bet: writing a six-page narrative forces clear thinking in a way no PowerPoint deck can.

## When to use

- New product proposal
- Project kickoff
- Strategy review
- Deep-discussion meeting where leadership needs to absorb context before debating
- Anything where the substance is too rich for slides

## When not to use

- Quick status update → use Default mode
- Decision documentation → use Decision Doc Mode
- Board / investor update → use Exec Comms Mode
- Anything <1 page in length → mode is overkill

## Structural requirements

In addition to the core 14 criteria, a Memo Mode draft must satisfy:

### M1. Press Release first (Critical for Memo Mode)

Page 1 is a press release announcing the product or initiative *as if it launched yesterday*. The press release answers: what did we ship, who is the customer, what's the headline benefit, and what's the customer quote that captures it.

**Test:** Could you copy page 1, drop it into a real press release template, and have it work? If the page describes a project rather than a product launch, fail.

### M2. Narrative, not bullets

Prose paragraphs, full sentences, complete thoughts. Bullets allowed only for genuinely list-like content (e.g., a list of three customer segments). Never bullet your argument.

**Test:** Read aloud. If the doc reads like a slide deck, rewrite as paragraphs.

### M3. Six pages (or thereabouts)

Target 4–6 pages. Anything shorter is too thin to force clear thinking. Anything longer means the writer didn't decide what mattered.

### M4. FAQ on the actual hard questions

Pages 2–6 are an FAQ, but the questions must be the ones a skeptical reader would actually ask. Soft-ball FAQs are tells that the writer hasn't pressure-tested the proposal.

**Examples of real FAQ questions:**
- "What happens if our largest customer doesn't adopt this?"
- "Why are we doing this now and not next year?"
- "What's the case for not doing this?"
- "What would change our mind?"

**Examples of soft-ball FAQ questions to flag and rewrite:**
- "How does this benefit our customers?"
- "What are the next steps?"
- "How does this align with our mission?"

### M5. Section headlines summarize

Each section heading carries the section's argument, not its topic. ("Three reasons we should ship in Q3" not "Timing considerations.")

### M6. Time investment

A real Amazon memo takes a week, not an afternoon. If the draft was produced in <1 day, expect it to fail M1 or M4.

## Quote (the source rule)

Bezos memo to senior staff (2004): "There is no way to write a six-page narratively structured memo and not have clear thinking. The reason writing a good 4 page memo is harder than 'writing' a 20 page powerpoint is because the narrative structure of a good memo forces better thought and better understanding of what's more important than what, and how things are related."
